Amateur Radio

This hobby dragged me in with the fact that you're basically turning tinkering with pliers into a piece of primitave technology you can use to talk to anyone you want. Also because Hams have a knack for giving away free coffee and donuts and tend to be just the most badass people. My callsign is KE5OTE, or phonetically "kilo-echo-five-oscar-tango-echo", which is the namesake of this website. I am a federally FCC licensed amateur radio operator, general class.

The details of my radio rig and a good portion of my ham radio career are available on qrz.com/db/ke5ote . In short, I currently have my own (free) yaesu transciever, (free) power supply, and (free) handmade antenna. I mostly operate phone ssb and follow up with handmade qsl cards.

Bones and Pelts

I have a particular interest in cleaning and whitening found/hunted animal bones, as well as collecting pelts. This also means I have more than one rotting dead animal in my room. Luckily, I have recived a volunteering position at the Austin Nature and Science Center so that I have access to far more specimens donated by the bartering-style trade counter. ANSC gets fresh, clean bones from me and I get hours of rubbing my hands raw with wire brushes.

Construction

Construction and working with power tools is pretty common throughout my hobbies, but this is mainly a section for woodwork. There is literally nothing more excerising of autonomy then being able to construct your own shelter and furniture.

One of my largest upcoming projects is going to be my own ham radio shack in my backyard that I plan on making myself (with the help of my girlfriend and my neighbors).

Itenerary for the KE5OTE Shack (as of 11/11):

  • Measuring 120ft^2 (12ft by 10ft)
  • Budget of $1,200
  • Made with reclaimed materials and doors/windows off facebook marketplace to cut costs
  • Elevated 6in off the ground with pavers (gravel foundation underneath, galvanized wire for pest control)
  • Frame will be exposed inside, exterior and floor made out of plywood
  • Corrugated metal roof, sloped towards the front of the shed
  • Interior built-ins like a workbench and shelves
  • Outdoor-rated buried extension cord from the main house
  • Decorated with a Texas flag, deer skulls, a leather chair, rug to protect the plywood, and all my ham radio equipment.

All amateur radio related additions like antennas and setups will be covered on the ham radio page.
